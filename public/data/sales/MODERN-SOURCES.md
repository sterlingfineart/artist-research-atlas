# Historic Art Sales — source notes

Every record in `sales.json` rests on public sources only (no Artprice data in any form). Basis key: **House** = the auction house's own page/press release read directly; **Press ×2+** = two or more independent reputable reports agreeing. Hammer and price-with-premium are recorded as separate fields throughout and never blended. Compiled 19 November 2026.

---

## 1. Vincent van Gogh — Portrait of Dr Gachet (1890)
Christie's, New York, 15 May 1990 — **Press ×2+** (no Christie's lot page for 1990 is published online)

- Artnet News, "10 Game-Changing Auctions": "Prior to the sale, the most expensive work sold at auction was van Gogh's 1890 *Portrait of Doctor Gachet*, which was sold to a Japanese billionaire for $82.5 million in 1990 at Christie's."
  https://news.artnet.com/market/10-game-changing-auctions-52082
- Premium basis, stated exactly as the source gives it (Wikipedia, "Portrait of Dr. Gachet", citing U.S. News): the picture "fetched a record price of $82.5 million (**$75 million, plus a 10 percent buyer's commission**)" — hence hammer 75,000,000 and price-with-premium 82,500,000.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portrait_of_Dr._Gachet
- Estimate: Christie's president Christopher Burge "had estimated the painting at between 40 and 50 million dollars" (Plundered Art / HARP research series); the Sotheby's Institute lecture record likewise gives "est: $40-50 million".
  https://plunderedart.org/2015/08/24/dr-gachet-from-confiscation-to-exile/
- Lot number: not established from a published source; recorded as null.

## 2. Peter Paul Rubens — The Massacre of the Innocents (c. 1611)
Sotheby's, London, 10 July 2002, Old Master Paintings Part One (sale L02111), lot 6 — **House + Press ×2**

- Sotheby's own corporate history page: "Rubens' *The Massacre of the Innocents* achieved £49.5 million in 2002, which remains a record for an Old Master painting."
  https://www.sothebys.com/en/about/our-history/
- Exact premium-inclusive figure: Getty Images' contemporary news caption (10/07/02): the painting "was sold for 49,506,650. It was expected to reach between 4 to 6 million" — the source of the estimate range recorded (£4–6 million).
  https://www.gettyimages.dk/detail/news-photo/sothebys-senior-old-masters-specialist-george-gordon-views-news-photo/830834946
- Contemporary trade report, Antiques and the Arts Weekly (16 July 2002): "sold for £49.5 million, making it the most expensive painting ever sold in pounds… a record for an Old Master painting at auction."
  https://antiquesandthearts.com/but-wait-another-record-sothebys-old-master-auction-raises-the-bar-for-the-entire-category/
- Hammer £45,000,000: reported as the hammer on the day by the Antiques Trade Gazette (Sotheby's publishes no hammer for this lot); documented in the price history compiled at iconotheca.com.
  https://iconotheca.com/artworks/peter-paul-rubens/rubens-the-massacre-of-the-innocents/

## 3. Pablo Picasso — Garçon à la pipe (1905)
Sotheby's, New York, 5 May 2004, sale N07989 (Greentree Foundation / Whitney Collection), lot 7 — **House**

- Sotheby's ecatalogue lot page (sale N07989, lot 7) is the lot of record; a later Sotheby's lot essay captions the picture "sold: Sotheby's May 2004, $104,168,000".
  http://www.sothebys.com/en/auctions/ecatalogue/2004/property-of-the-greentree-foundation-from-the-collection-of-mr-and-mrs-john-hay-whitney-impressionist-and-modern-art-n07989/lot.7.html
- Sotheby's press release as published on the night (artdaily): "Pablo Picasso's Garçon à la Pipe, from the fabled Whitney Collection, sold for **$104,168,000** to an anonymous buyer, making it the most expensive painting in the world."
  https://artdaily.cc/news/10235/Sotheby-s-Sells-World-s-Most-Expensive-Painting
- Hammer and estimate (Associated Press report, 6 May 2004): "The total includes the auction price of **$93 million** plus the auction house's commission of about $11 million… Sotheby's called the work, which had a presale estimate of **$70 million**…"
  https://vindyarchives.com/news/2004/may/06/picasso-painting-breaks-auction-record/?print
- Antiques and the Arts Weekly's sale report confirms: "it realized $104,168,000, **including premium**."
  https://antiquesandthearts.com/a-high-water-mark/
- **Discrepancy flagged:** a figure of $104,211,200 appears in the commissioning brief for this dataset. No source found states it; the house and every contemporary report give $104,168,000, which is what is recorded.

## 4. Leonardo da Vinci (attributed to) — Salvator Mundi (c. 1500)

  https://www.christies.com/en/stories/leonardo-and-post-war-results-new-york-ad70dd5889e64989a76c94e4358c760d
- Estimate, Christie's offering release (10 October 2017): "The estimate is **in the region of $100 million US**."
  https://press.christies.com/press-release-christies-to-offer-the-last-leonardo-da-vinci-painting-in-private-hands/

## 5. Claude Monet — Meules (1890)
Sotheby's, New York, 14 May 2019, Impressionist & Modern Art Evening Sale, lot 8 — **House + lot-level results reporting**

- Sotheby's sale report: "Claude Monet's *Meules* was the evening's undoubted winner, with a winning bid of **$110,747,000**. The sale sets a new artist record price for a work by the artist… the first work of Impressionist Art to sell for more than $100 million at auction."
  https://www.sothebys.com/en/articles/record-breaking-110-7-million-monet-shatters-records-at-impressionist-modern-art-evening-sale?_amp=true
- Guinness World Records: "sold for **$110,747,000** (£85,227,200; €98,572,600), **including buyer's premium**, by Sotheby's in New York, USA, on 14 May 2019."
  https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/62466-most-expensive-painting-by-monet
- Lot-level data (The Value's results table for the sale): Lot 8; "Estimate: in excess of US$55,000,000; **Hammer price: US$97,000,000; Price realised: US$110,747,000**."
  https://en.thevalue.com/articles/monet-haystacks-meules-world-record-impressionist-art
- **Discrepancy flagged:** a figure of $110,747,045 appears in the commissioning brief. No source found states it; Sotheby's and Guinness both give $110,747,000, which is what is recorded.

## 6. Rembrandt van Rijn — The Standard Bearer (1636)
Private sale, completed January 2022 — **Government/museum reporting + Guinness**

- Guinness World Records: "*The Standard Bearer* (1636), by Dutch master Rembrandt van Rijn (1606–69) was **bought from the Rothschild banking family for €175 million** ($198.9 million; £146.1 million), **as confirmed on 19 January 2022**. Of this total, the Dutch state contributed €150 million… with the remainder provided by the Rembrandt Association and the Rijksmuseum Fund."
  https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/646014-most-expensive-painting-by-rembrandt-in-private-sale
- The Burlington Magazine corroborates the funding split: "acquired for the Rijksmuseum for €175 million. The Dutch government provided the lion's share – €150 million – while the remainder of the required funds came from the Rembrandt Association and the Rijksmuseum Fund" (the Association €15 million and the Fund €10 million, per the Rijksmuseum's reporting).
  https://www.burlington.org.uk/archive/editorial/national-collecting
- Sale date recorded as 2022-01-19, the confirmation date given by Guinness; the Dutch government's intention to buy had been announced in December 2021. A private treaty sale has no hammer/premium split, so the price is carried in `price_as_reported` only.

  http://phillips.com/auction/NY010724

  https://www.TheArtNewspaper.com/2024/11/19/phillips-new-york-contemporary-art-evening-auction-44m-pollock-twombly-basquiat
- Artnet News: "an untitled Jackson Pollock from around 1948, which went for $15.3 million, just above its $13 million estimate."

This is noted in the record so the 2024 figure is not read as a settled, paid price.
  https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2026/05/20/phillips-new-york-auction-john-loeb-tina-hills-report

## 8. Gustav Klimt — Portrait of Elisabeth Lederer (1914–1916)
Sotheby's, New York, 18 November 2025, Leonard A. Lauder, Collector Evening Auction, lot 8 — **Press ×2+ (lot-level)**

- The Value's lot-level results table: "Lot 8 | Gustav Klimt | *Bildnis Elisabeth Lederer (Portrait of Elisabeth Lederer)*… Estimate Upon Request (Expected to fetch in the region of US$150 million); **Hammer Price: US$205,000,000; Sold: US$236,360,000**."
  https://en.thevalue.com/articles/sothebys-ny-leonard-lauder-collection-gustav-klimt-munch-2025-autumn-auction-result
- The Art Newspaper: the portrait "sold for a hammer price of $205m (**$236.3m with fees**)" and "is now the second-most valuable work of art ever sold at auction."
  https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2025/11/18/klimt-record-sothebys-new-york-lauder-collection
- Artnet News year-end list: "Estimate: $150 million; Final Price: $236.3 million; Sotheby's New York, November 18."
  https://news.artnet.com/market/10-top-lots-auction-2025-2723777
- **Rounding disagreement flagged:** most press (AP-derived coverage, Wikipedia) rounds the with-fees figure to **$236.4 million**, and one Sotheby's Realty article gives a "final purchase price of $236.34 million". The exact lot-level figure published in results tables is **$236,360,000**, which is what is recorded; the record's `price_as_reported` notes the common $236.4 million rounding.

- Sotheby's lot page states "**Lot Sold — 47,830,000 USD**". The page gives no "price realised" wording and nowhere states whether the figure includes buyer's premium. In line with the dataset's rules the figure is recorded **exactly as posted**, in `price_as_reported` only, with `hammer` and `price_with_premium` left null; the ambiguity is flagged in the record itself and is **not** resolved here.
  https://www.sothebys.com/en/buy/auction/2026/modern-evening-auction-4/the-martha-mckeen-of-wellfleet-2

- Press corroboration of the result level: ARTnews's report of the evening (via syndication) records "Edward Hopper's *The Martha Mckeen of Wellfleet* sold for $47.8 million" in the Modern Evening Auction, which followed the Blaquier-Arrieta sale.
  https://arc-codex.com/article/caff6f0c1fcaa0513ab7e7dca06facac
- A local report from the sale room (Good Morning Gloucester) claimed a $41 million hammer; that figure could not be reconciled with Sotheby's posted result under the house's published premium tiers and is **not** used.

## Discrepancies and open points (summary)

1. **Picasso, Garçon à la pipe:** brief figure $104,211,200 not found in any source; house and all contemporary reports give **$104,168,000** with premium (hammer $93,000,000). Recorded: $104,168,000.
2. **Monet, Meules:** brief figure $110,747,045 not found in any source; Sotheby's and Guinness give **$110,747,000** with premium (hammer $97,000,000). Recorded: $110,747,000.
3. **Klimt, Portrait of Elisabeth Lederer:** exact with-fees figure published in results tables as **$236,360,000**; most press rounds to $236.4 million, The Art Newspaper and Artnet to $236.3 million, and one Sotheby's Realty article gives $236.34 million. Recorded: $236,360,000, with the rounding noted in the record.
4. **Van Gogh, Châtaigniers en fleurs:** major outlets report the with-fees figure rounded ($190.4 million); the exact **$190,430,000** rests on sale-room reporting (Particle News) consistent with the sale's published with-fees total. Hammer $165,000,000 is unanimous.
5. **Hopper, Martha McKeen:** Sotheby's posts "Lot Sold — 47,830,000 USD" without stating the premium basis; recorded as posted, unresolved by design.
6. **Pollock, Untitled (c. 1948):** the November 2024 Phillips figure ($15,335,000 with premium; hammer $13,000,000) is the house's own recorded result, but the buyer subsequently defaulted (The Art Newspaper, May 2026) and the work was re-offered in 2026 at $9.1 million with fees. Both facts are carried in the record's note.
7. Nothing was dropped: all seven seed sales and all three suggested additions (Rubens 2002, Rembrandt 2022, Klimt 2025) verified and are included — 10 records in total.
